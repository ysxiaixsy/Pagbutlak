# Evaluation of pagbutlak.org

*Short on time? There's a TL;DR summary at the end.*

Pagbutlak's readers mostly find its stories on Facebook and Instagram, so pagbutlak.org has a different job. Pagbutlak itself describes the site as "a digital outlet and central archive of all publication content": the one place where every story is kept, organized, and searchable on a platform Pagbutlak controls. Looking at the site with that job in mind, one pattern runs through almost every problem I found. The site stays accurate only as long as people remember to do things by hand: shrink photos before uploading, update the menu when a section is added or emptied, remove "LIVE" from a headline once an event ends, pick a category, type the writer's name into the article, and copy coverage from Facebook to the site. Staff are busy and change every year, so these steps get skipped. Most of my recommendations make the system remember for them. This matters more than it seems. Pagbutlak's Twitter/X account has been suspended since December 2025, and everything posted only there went with it. The website is the one copy no platform can take away.

## Strengths

The site has a solid technical foundation. According to Google's PageSpeed Insights test of the homepage on September 19, 2026 ([full report](https://pagespeed.web.dev/analysis/https-pagbutlak-org/6r9zg7x4kv?form_factor=mobile&category=performance&category=accessibility&category=best-practices&category=seo&category=agentic-browsing&hl=en-US)), which is the source of the speed numbers in this evaluation, the server responds in about 10 milliseconds, and the page does very little JavaScript work (20 milliseconds of "blocking time" on mobile, where anything under 200 is considered good), so once content arrives, the page responds to taps and clicks right away. Google's automated checks give it perfect scores of 100 for Accessibility, Best Practices, and SEO, meaning the code follows the standard rules for screen readers, security, and search engines. On desktop, everything works as expected.

Underneath, the content is better organized than the menu suggests. WordPress already stores a real hierarchy of sections: News has Live, News Bytes, and In-Depth; Features has Banwa Narratives, Kampus, Essay, and Profiles; Opinion has Column, First Person, and Editorial. The hosting plan also includes two tools the site isn't fully using yet: an image service (i0.wp.com) that resizes photos on request, and a public data feed (the REST API at /wp-json/) that lets other pages and apps read the site's stories. I used that feed to build the mockup. Finally, the live coverage of #GASC61 and #Tapatan 2026 shows a newsroom that can report in real time.

## Points for improvement

### Photos make the site heavy

A first visit to the homepage downloads about 6 MB on mobile and 7 MB on desktop (desktop shows two more photos). About 80% of that comes from just two uploaded images: a 2.6 MB graphic and a 2.3 MB photo whose file name suggests it came straight from a camera. Google's test flags about 97% of each file as unnecessary, because the photo is displayed much smaller than it was uploaded. Article pages have the same problem. The header image on the Bontok column is a 4.3 MB file, but the same image resized to 800 pixels wide through the site's own image service is 0.45 MB, about 90% smaller, and looks the same on a phone. More than half of the 29 Opinion and Features header images I checked are over 2 MB. This isn't one mistake. It happens every time someone uploads a photo without resizing it first. For readers on prepaid data or weak signal, 6 MB loads slowly and costs real money. (Google's lab test estimates the main headline takes 18.8 seconds to appear, but that simulates a slow mobile connection. The page weight is the more reliable measure.)

The fix is to make resizing automatic. The site can serve every photo through i0.wp.com with a width setting and a `srcset`, a list of sizes that lets the browser pick the right one: a phone gets a phone-sized photo, and a large screen gets a larger one. Alternatively, an optimization setting can shrink photos as they're uploaded. Readers who want the full-resolution version can still open it with WordPress's built-in "Expand on click" option on article pages. Fonts need a smaller fix in the same spirit. The site's fonts are six `.ttf` files with no `font-display` rule, so text can stay invisible while the fonts download and then shift the layout when they arrive. Switching to `.woff2` (a compressed font format) with `font-display: swap` shows text immediately in a backup font and swaps in the real one when it's ready. Google's test also estimates about a second of delay from style and script files that WordPress.com bundles and loads before the page appears. Those are hard to change on this hosting plan, so photos and fonts are the fixes that give the most for the effort.

### The menu has drifted from the content

The top menu is nine hand-typed links, and it no longer matches what the site holds. News Bytes, a subsection of News, has a top-level spot. From the Newsroom, with five posts, isn't in the menu at all. Sports has zero posts but keeps its place, so clicking it opens a blank page. "News" leads to a separate page instead of the News section, and subsections like Kampus and Banwa Narratives can only be reached by clicking a small label on an article. Nobody made a bad decision here. A hand-typed menu simply goes out of date when nobody remembers to update it. I recommend building the menu from the site's category data with a few rules: show the main sections in a fixed order, put subsections under their parent, and hide sections that are empty or Uncategorized. Then the menu updates itself whenever editors add a section or empty one.

The menu also doesn't show where you are. The current section should be highlighted and marked with `aria-current`, a code attribute that tells screen readers "you are here." On phones, the nine links wrap onto several lines and fill about a third of the first screen, pushing the news down, and the search box in the header isn't sized for the screen. In the mockup, I put the main sections in a single row that scrolls sideways and moved subsections and search into a full-screen menu behind one button. Every section stays one tap away without taking over the screen. The header also scrolls away with the page, so readers have to go back to the top to search or switch sections. A "partially persistent" header, one that hides while you scroll down and returns as soon as you scroll up, keeps the menu in reach without covering the article.

### Labels and previews should tell the truth

Some labels are typed by hand, so they go out of date. "LIVE:" is written into headlines even though the site already has a Live category, and the two already disagree: one live update from #GASC61 is filed under Issues only. The labels also never expire, so the #Tapatan post still says LIVE days after the forum ended. If the badge is generated from the Live category and the time of the last update, it can say "Live now" during an event and "Live coverage ended" afterward, without anyone editing old headlines.

Story previews have a similar problem. WordPress cuts each excerpt automatically from the article's opening lines, so previews stop mid-sentence, and live posts preview as a string of timestamps ("5:17 PM, August 6 5:21 PM, August 6 …"). A one-sentence summary written by the editor fixes both. The homepage also lists the same six stories twice, under "Top stories" and again under "Latest Stories," using space that could show Opinion, Features, or Kultura. And one post is still Uncategorized, the label WordPress applies when nobody picks a section.

### Each story should be one link

On the homepage, each story's photo and headline are separate links to the same article, and in one of the two lists the date is a third. Hovering underlines the headline, but the photo gives no feedback even though it's a link. Keyboard users have to press Tab two or three times per story, and screen readers read each headline twice, because the photo's description is the headline itself. Since every story is also listed twice, a single article gets five links on the homepage. Making the whole preview one link, with one hover effect and one focus outline, is simpler for everyone.

### Search should help readers find stories

Search works, but it shows results only after you press Enter and gives no suggestions as you type. Showing matching headlines while the reader types lets them recognize a story instead of remembering its exact wording. The results page also shows a second search box in the page body. A single box, already filled in with the reader's search, is less confusing.

### The site only plans for when everything works

Every page should tell readers what's happening and what they can do next, even when there's nothing to show or something goes wrong. Designers call these empty, loading, and error states, and the site leaves most of them blank. The Sports section has no stories, and its page shows nothing at all, not even a line saying so. It should say that no stories have been published yet and point readers to the latest news. Search is the one place that gets this partly right: a search with no matches says so, but it leaves the reader at a dead end instead of suggesting a section or recent stories. On short pages like these, the footer also floats up the screen instead of staying at the bottom, which a small layout change fixes.

Photos need the same planning. When a post has no photo, its preview simply has no image, so it doesn't line up with the ones around it. When a photo is slow or fails to load, which is likely with multi-megabyte files on a weak connection, the reader sees an empty space where the photo should be. Every photo slot should have a fallback: a soft placeholder color while the image loads, a Pagbutlak-branded block when a post has no photo, and a short description (alt text) so that even a photo that fails still says what it showed, which article photos currently lack. These fixes follow two of Nielsen's usability heuristics, the most widely used rules of thumb in interface design: always show people what's happening, and help them recover when something goes wrong. The mockup shows each case: placeholder cards while stories load, a "Stories couldn't load" message with a Try again button, a "No stories in this section yet" message with a way back, a Pagbutlak block for stories without a usable photo, and a placeholder color instead of a broken-image icon when a photo fails.

### Writers deserve their own pages

Almost every article (99 of 102) is published under one shared account, "Pagbutlak UP Visayas CAS," and writers' names are typed into the body of each article. A reader who likes a column can't click the columnist's name to find their other work, and search engines see one author for everything. Giving each writer a profile, either a WordPress account or a byline field on each post (a guest-author plugin can do this without separate logins and handles pen names like "Autarkeia"), would create a page for every writer automatically. For student journalists, that page is also a ready-made portfolio. Separate accounts would also make the site safer. If staff share the password to one account, every member who leaves still has it. Individual accounts with limited roles (a writer can draft, an editor can publish) mean access can be removed when someone graduates, and the site keeps a record of who changed what.

### Photos and videos have no home on the site

Pagbutlak's photo coverage and other visual work appear on Facebook and Instagram, but the site has no place for them. There is no Multimedia section, so the archive is missing the visual record of events, and photographers have no page to point to. I recommend a Multimedia section for photo essays, galleries, and videos, with a caption and a photographer credit on every image. WordPress's built-in gallery and video blocks can do this without new tools. Until the section has stories, it should tell readers where to find the work for now, which is what the mockup's Multimedia page does.

### The archive is incomplete

The homepage's latest stories jump from August 8 to September 14, 2026, and coverage from that gap, such as the First Day Rage on August 17, seems to exist only on Facebook. The newest Opinion piece on the site is from May 7 and the newest Features piece from March 24, so neither section has had a new story on the site since the academic year began. Chrome's real-user data also doesn't have enough visits to report on the site, which fits a readership that stays on social media. This is the most important issue, because it's the one that gets harder to fix over time. I recommend publishing every story on the site first and linking social posts back to it. That builds the archive and brings Facebook readers to the site, where they can find related stories. The footer also still links to the suspended Twitter/X account. Until it's restored, the link should be removed, because every link should lead somewhere real.

### Accessibility goes beyond the score

The perfect accessibility score means the site passed every automated check, not that it's fully accessible. Google's report lists ten checks that need a person, such as whether keyboard focus is visible and moves in a logical order, and those haven't been tested yet. There's also at least one gap the checker can't see. Header photos on article pages have an empty description (alt text), so screen-reader users get no idea what the photo shows. An empty description is technically valid, so the score stays at 100. A pre-publish checklist plugin can block publishing until the photo has a description, and a quick keyboard and screen-reader test would close the rest of the gap between the score and the real experience.

## Where to start

Not everything has to happen at once. Some fixes take minutes: remove the dead Twitter/X link, hide the empty Sports section, point "News" to the News section, and write a one-sentence excerpt for each new post. The most urgent work is the archive, because it gets harder to fix the longer it waits: publish every new story on the site first, catch up on the coverage missing since August, and turn on automatic photo resizing so each new upload stops adding to the problem. After that come the structural changes shown in the mockup (the menu built from the site's sections, the mobile navigation, live badges, one link per story, and search suggestions), which can all be done in the theme without leaving WordPress. Writer pages and a Multimedia section can follow once the staff agree on how bylines and photo credits should work. Finally, a short handbook for the web team (how to upload photos, how sections work, how to run live coverage) would keep these fixes from being forgotten when the staff changes next year.

## Closing

The mockup at https://pagbutlak-mockup.vercel.app puts these recommendations into practice: a menu generated from the site's own sections, a marker for the current section, a header that returns when you scroll up, one link per story, a live badge that updates itself, suggestions as you type, pages that explain themselves when there's nothing to show or something fails to load, and photos resized through the image service the site already has. Its section pages and search load real stories from the site's public data feed, fixing typed "LIVE" labels and cut-off excerpts along the way. None of it requires leaving WordPress. The goal is a site that keeps itself accurate, so the staff can spend their time on reporting, and an archive that stays complete no matter what happens on social media.

## TL;DR

- **Main idea:** The site stays accurate only if people remember to do things by hand: resize photos, update the menu, remove "LIVE" labels, pick categories, and copy coverage from Facebook. Most of my recommendations make the system remember instead.
- **What works:** A fast server, very little JavaScript, perfect automated scores for Accessibility, Best Practices, and SEO, and a well-organized back end with an image service and a data feed the site isn't fully using yet.
- **Photos:** The homepage downloads about 6 MB, and 80% of that is two photos uploaded at full size. Resize every photo automatically through the site's own image service, and switch the fonts to a lighter format.
- **Navigation:** The hand-typed menu is out of date, doesn't show where you are, and fills about a third of a phone screen. Build it from the site's sections, use one swipeable row plus a full-screen menu, and bring the header back when readers scroll up.
- **Labels and previews:** "LIVE" labels never expire, excerpts stop mid-sentence, the homepage lists stories twice, and each story has several links. Generate the live badge automatically, write one-sentence excerpts, and make each preview one link.
- **When things go wrong:** Empty sections show a blank page, searches with no matches dead-end, search gives no suggestions, and missing or slow photos leave gaps. Every page should say what's happening and what to do next.
- **The archive:** Coverage since August exists only on Facebook, there's no Multimedia section, almost every post comes from one shared account, and the footer links to a suspended Twitter/X account. Publish on the site first, add a Multimedia section and writer pages, and give staff their own accounts.
- **Accessibility:** A perfect score isn't the same as tested. Article photos have no descriptions, and ten manual checks haven't been done.
- **Where to start:** Quick fixes first, then the archive and automatic photo resizing, then the changes shown in the mockup, then writer pages and Multimedia, and finally a short handbook so the fixes survive staff turnover.
