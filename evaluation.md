# Evaluation of pagbutlak.org

Pagbutlak's readers mostly find its stories on Facebook and Instagram, so pagbutlak.org has a different job. Pagbutlak itself describes the site as "a digital outlet and central archive of all publication content": the one place where every story is kept, organized, and searchable on a platform Pagbutlak controls. Looking at the site with that job in mind, one pattern runs through almost every problem I found. The site stays accurate only as long as people remember to do things by hand: shrink photos before uploading, update the menu when a section is added or emptied, remove "LIVE" from a headline once an event ends, pick a category, type the writer's name into the article, and copy coverage from Facebook to the site. Staff are busy and change every year, so these steps get skipped. Most of my recommendations make the system remember for them. This matters more than it seems. Pagbutlak's Twitter/X account has been suspended since December 2025, and everything posted only there went with it. The website is the one copy no platform can take away.

## Strengths

The site has a solid technical foundation. The server responds in about 10 milliseconds, and the page does very little JavaScript work (20 milliseconds of "blocking time" on mobile, where anything under 200 is considered good), so once content arrives, the page responds to taps and clicks right away. Google's automated checks give it perfect scores of 100 for Accessibility, Best Practices, and SEO, meaning the code follows the standard rules for screen readers, security, and search engines. On desktop, everything works as expected.

Underneath, the content is better organized than the menu suggests. WordPress already stores a real hierarchy of sections: News has Live, News Bytes, and In-Depth; Features has Banwa Narratives, Kampus, Essay, and Profiles; Opinion has Column, First Person, and Editorial. The hosting plan also includes two tools the site isn't fully using yet: an image service (i0.wp.com) that resizes photos on request, and a public data feed (the REST API at /wp-json/) that lets other pages and apps read the site's stories. I used that feed to build the mockup. Finally, the live coverage of #GASC61 and #Tapatan 2026 shows a newsroom that can report in real time.

## Points for improvement

### Photos make the site heavy

A first visit to the homepage downloads about 6 MB on mobile and 7 MB on desktop (desktop shows two more photos). About 80% of that comes from just two uploaded images: a 2.6 MB graphic and a 2.3 MB photo whose file name suggests it came straight from a camera. Google's test flags about 97% of each file as unnecessary, because the photo is displayed much smaller than it was uploaded. Article pages have the same problem. The header image on the Bontok column is a 4.3 MB file, but the same image resized to 800 pixels wide through the site's own image service is 0.45 MB, about 90% smaller, and looks the same on a phone. More than half of the 29 Opinion and Features header images I checked are over 2 MB. This isn't one mistake. It happens every time someone uploads a photo without resizing it first. For readers on prepaid data or weak signal, 6 MB loads slowly and costs real money. (Google's lab test estimates the main headline takes 18.8 seconds to appear, but that simulates a slow mobile connection. The page weight is the more reliable measure.)

The fix is to make resizing automatic. The site can serve every photo through i0.wp.com with a width setting and a `srcset`, a list of sizes that lets the browser pick the right one: a phone gets a phone-sized photo, and a large screen gets a larger one. Alternatively, an optimization setting can shrink photos as they're uploaded. Readers who want the full-resolution version can still open it with WordPress's built-in "Expand on click" option on article pages. Fonts need a smaller fix in the same spirit. The site's fonts are six `.ttf` files with no `font-display` rule, so text can stay invisible while the fonts download and then shift the layout when they arrive. Switching to `.woff2` (a compressed font format) with `font-display: swap` shows text immediately in a backup font and swaps in the real one when it's ready.

### The menu has drifted from the content

The top menu is nine hand-typed links, and it no longer matches what the site holds. News Bytes, a subsection of News, has a top-level spot. From the Newsroom, with five posts, isn't in the menu at all. Sports has zero posts but keeps its place, so clicking it opens a blank page. "News" leads to a separate page instead of the News section, and subsections like Kampus and Banwa Narratives can only be reached by clicking a small label on an article. Nobody made a bad decision here. A hand-typed menu simply goes out of date when nobody remembers to update it. I recommend building the menu from the site's category data with a few rules: show the main sections in a fixed order, put subsections under their parent, and hide sections that are empty or Uncategorized. Then the menu updates itself whenever editors add a section or empty one.

The menu also doesn't show where you are. The current section should be highlighted and marked with `aria-current`, a code attribute that tells screen readers "you are here." On phones, the nine links wrap onto several lines and fill about a third of the first screen, pushing the news down, and the search box in the header isn't sized for the screen. In the mockup, I put the main sections in a single row that scrolls sideways and moved subsections and search into a full-screen menu behind one button. Every section stays one tap away without taking over the screen. The header also scrolls away with the page, so readers have to go back to the top to search or switch sections. A "partially persistent" header, one that hides while you scroll down and returns as soon as you scroll up, keeps the menu in reach without covering the article.

### Labels and previews should tell the truth

Some labels are typed by hand, so they go out of date. "LIVE:" is written into headlines even though the site already has a Live category, and the two already disagree: one live update from #GASC61 is filed under Issues only. The labels also never expire, so the #Tapatan post still says LIVE days after the forum ended. If the badge is generated from the Live category and the time of the last update, it can say "Live now" during an event and "Live coverage ended" afterward, without anyone editing old headlines.

Story previews have a similar problem. WordPress cuts each excerpt automatically from the article's opening lines, so previews stop mid-sentence, and live posts preview as a string of timestamps ("5:17 PM, August 6 5:21 PM, August 6 …"). A one-sentence summary written by the editor fixes both. The homepage also lists the same six stories twice, under "Top stories" and again under "Latest Stories," using space that could show Opinion, Features, or Kultura. And one post is still Uncategorized, the label WordPress applies when nobody picks a section.

### Each story should be one link

On the homepage, each story's photo and headline are separate links to the same article, and in one of the two lists the date is a third. Hovering underlines the headline, but the photo gives no feedback even though it's a link. Keyboard users have to press Tab two or three times per story, and screen readers read each headline twice, because the photo's description is the headline itself. Since every story is also listed twice, a single article gets five links on the homepage. Making the whole preview one link, with one hover effect and one focus outline, is simpler for everyone.

### Search and empty pages should help readers keep going

Search works, but it shows results only after you press Enter and gives no suggestions as you type. Showing matching headlines while the reader types lets them recognize a story instead of remembering its exact wording. The results page also shows a second search box in the page body. A single box, already filled in with the reader's search, is less confusing. Empty pages should explain themselves: the Sports section shows nothing at all, when it could say that no stories have been published yet and point readers to the latest news. On short pages like that, the footer also floats up the screen instead of staying at the bottom, which a small layout change fixes.

### Writers deserve their own pages

Almost every article (99 of 102) is published under one shared account, "Pagbutlak UP Visayas CAS," and writers' names are typed into the body of each article. A reader who likes a column can't click the columnist's name to find their other work, and search engines see one author for everything. Giving each writer a profile, either a WordPress account or a byline field on each post (a guest-author plugin can do this without separate logins and handles pen names like "Autarkeia"), would create a page for every writer automatically. For student journalists, that page is also a ready-made portfolio.

### The archive is incomplete

The homepage's latest stories jump from August 8 to September 14, 2026, and coverage from that gap, such as the First Day Rage on August 17, seems to exist only on Facebook. The newest Opinion piece on the site is from May 7 and the newest Features piece from March 24, so neither section has had a new story on the site since the academic year began. Chrome's real-user data also doesn't have enough visits to report on the site, which fits a readership that stays on social media. This is the most important issue, because it's the one that gets harder to fix over time. I recommend publishing every story on the site first and linking social posts back to it. That builds the archive and brings Facebook readers to the site, where they can find related stories. The footer also still links to the suspended Twitter/X account. Until it's restored, the link should be removed, because every link should lead somewhere real.

### Accessibility goes beyond the score

The perfect accessibility score means the site passed every automated check, not that it's fully accessible. Google's report lists ten checks that need a person, such as whether keyboard focus is visible and moves in a logical order, and those haven't been tested yet. There's also at least one gap the checker can't see. Header photos on article pages have an empty description (alt text), so screen-reader users get no idea what the photo shows. An empty description is technically valid, so the score stays at 100. A pre-publish checklist plugin can block publishing until the photo has a description, and a quick keyboard and screen-reader test would close the rest of the gap between the score and the real experience.

## Closing

The mockup at https://pagbutlak-mockup.vercel.app puts these recommendations into practice: a menu generated from the site's own sections, a marker for the current section, a header that returns when you scroll up, one link per story, a live badge that updates itself, suggestions as you type, empty pages that explain themselves, and photos resized through the image service the site already has. Its section pages and search load real stories from the site's public data feed, fixing typed "LIVE" labels and cut-off excerpts along the way. None of it requires leaving WordPress. The goal is a site that keeps itself accurate, so the staff can spend their time on reporting, and an archive that stays complete no matter what happens on social media.
